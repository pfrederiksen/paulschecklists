2026 Topps Mars Attacks Christmas combines a 50-card base set, an extended 25-character subset, and inserts covering the whole holiday invasion, including Battle Ready, Caught in the Crossfire Autographs, Cryptids Alliance, Headlines!, and Invasion Worn Relics.

Let’s take a closer look at the top cards to collect in 2026 Topps Mars Attacks Christmas.

## Base Set

The 50-card base set of Topps Mars Attacks Christmas tells one continuous story, keeping with the format established by the original set decades ago. This time, the Martian invasion crashes into the holidays.

The story of the base set anchors the whole checklist, with numbered parallels adding to the excitement and options, including the Candy Cane parallel numbered to 299. A 25-character subset spotlights standout figures from the story, adding to the narrative aspect of the set.

## Battle Ready

The Battle Ready insert imagines Santa and his friends trading in their hammers for hardware and becoming battle-hardened soldiers, ready to defend the North Pole rather than toy-making imps. It’s the kind of invasion Mars Attacks has long been known for, with Santa and his friends against the Martians, brought to life in a TCG-style design.

## Caught in the Crossfire Autographs

Caught in the Crossfire Autographs bring real celebrity signers into the middle of the invasion, including skateboarding legend Tony Hawk, shown here in a Red Refractor parallel. Other names on the checklist include Bill Nye, Brent Spiner, Mike Tyson, Will Smith, Chuck Norris, and Jeff Goldblum, giving collectors a wide array of ink to search for.

## Cryptids Alliance

Cryptids Alliance recruits the Earth’s most legendary creatures to join the fight against the Martians, from the Abominable Snowman to Krampus, the Tooth Fairy, and more. The insert reimagines folklore under the distinctive Mars Attacks style, giving each cryptid its own role in the holiday invasion.

## Headlines!

Headlines! mocks up newspaper front pages straight out of the invasion story, like this cover reporting a meteor strike on the North Pole. Each card interprets a different beat from the saga, adding to the high genre style and narrative hooks that have always defined Mars Attacks.

## Invasion Worn Relics

Invasion Worn Relics embed a physical swatch of material into the card itself, built around items connected to the set’s holiday battle. This example carries a swatch of wrapping paper, while others include items like “Yeti Fur,” “Santa’s Toy Bag,” or “Martian Meteor.”

## The History of Topps Mars Attacks

Mars Attacks began as a Topps trading card set in 1962, built around a series of gruesomely funny illustrations depicting a full-scale alien invasion of Earth. The set became a cult classic almost immediately, later inspiring the 1996 Tim Burton film and a long line of card revivals — from Heritage sets to Invasion and Occupation storylines.

Topps Mars Attacks Christmas continues that tradition, folding the franchise’s chaotic charm into a new holiday format for 2026.

## Who is Topps Mars Attacks® Christmas For?

## Why Does Topps Mars Attacks® Christmas Matter?

Mars Attacks has always thrived on contrast — gleeful destruction rendered in bright, playful pulp-art color. Putting that against a Christmas backdrop pushes the absurdity even further. This is a brand that’s never taken itself too seriously, and the legacy of parody and nostalgia are both alive and well in the new Christmas release.

Artist Jason Crosby has been helping create trading cards for over a decade. Beginning with the 2013 Topps Mars Attacks Invasion set, Crosby has created artwork cards for many Mars Attacks releases, including the exciting new insert Battle Ready in 2026 Topps Mars Attacks Christmas.

An artist and a collector, Jason is also a regular at his LCS, Top Dog Comics & Cards, developing a friendship with shop owner Chris Galloway over the years. The two have put on artist events at the shop, highlighting Crosby’s work on both Mars Attacks and Garbage Pail Kids.

DISCOVER TOPPS MARS ATTACKS CHRISTMAS

With the release of 2026 Topps Mars Attacks Christmas, Topps RIPPED sat down with Jason and Chris to talk about creating and collecting Mars Attacks, GPK, and other pop culture cards and memorabilia.

Mars Attacks artist Jason Crosby and Top Dog Comics & Cards owner Chris Galloway talk the new Battle Ready insert in 2026 Topps Mars Attacks Christmas, Crosby’s decade of Mars Attacks and Garbage Pail Kids artwork, and a thriving collector community in pop culture.

## Build the Mars Attacks Battle Ready Insert

“For Battle Ready, we wanted the cards to have a trading card game feel, with different characters battling for control of the North Pole,” Jason told RIPPED. His style brings the cards to life with the visual touches of a card game, while still carrying the overarching, absurd story of the set as Santa & co. fight a Martian invasion.

“The way I like to draw, it’s full of action, energy,” Jason said. “You would probably never hire me if you were looking for realistic portraits of Harrison Ford. It’s all movement and action and a little over the top. With Mars Attacks, and with Garbage Pail Kids, I think they really fit my style.

“With these Mars Attacks cards, you look at it and maybe somebody’s getting mauled, or shot at, but it’s not a frightening experience, it’s a fun one.”

The insert features Santa, Mrs. Claus, a Martian saucer landing, and more. “One of them is a direct play on a 1962 Mars Attacks card, Destroying a Dog,” Jason explained. “A Martian is zapping a little boy’s dog, so I did zapping a reindeer. Nostalgia, over-the-top, a little dark humor. That’s what Mars Attacks is all about.”

## Mars Attacks Through the Years

Jason’s work at Topps has seen him creating artwork for a number of releases and franchises.

“For 2013 Topps Mars Attacks, it was the first time I drew sketch cards along with having a base card featured,” he said. “I’ve had the chance to work on several sets over the years.

“There was a nine-card sports card/Martian crossover set from the 2015 Mars Attacks: Occupation, called Mars Attacks Superstars, where I took classic Topps sports designs and meshed them with Martians playing gory versions of our favorite sports. Some characters were ‘Mickey Mangle, ‘Babe Ruthless,’ and ‘Bobby Gore.'”
